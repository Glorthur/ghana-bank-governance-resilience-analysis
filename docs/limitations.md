# Limitations

- The panel is small: 16 banks and at most six years, with only 10 banks showing any within-bank ORI variation.
- ORI is a disclosure index derived from annual-report evidence. It is not a direct observation of disruption, recovery time, or realised operational resilience.
- The 100-point ceiling affects identification: 51 of 95 primary observations are at 100, and the within-bank ORI standard deviation is 7.357.
- Fixed-effects estimates identify within-bank associations and do not establish causality. Omitted time-varying factors, measurement error, and selection into reporting remain possible.
- CAR and NPL use smaller, non-randomly available samples. NPL is a distinct outcome and must not be presented as an ORI proxy.
- H3 is weakly identified: its clustered standard error is 18.512, and the interaction confidence interval includes zero. Simple slopes and sensitivity estimates should not be described as definitive moderation evidence.
- Results from pooled, random-effects, Driscoll–Kraay, or nonlinear specifications answer different questions and are not substitutes for the planned bank- and year-FE model. Driscoll–Kraay is especially unreliable with T=6.
- Bank age is not included because a complete, independently verified bank-year measure was not assembled.
